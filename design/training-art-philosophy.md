# Painted Ground

A plan drawing of a place where nothing is fixed except the paint. Painted Ground treats a flat surface as a record of everything that has happened on it: lines put down with care, then worn by rubber, then put down again. The image is a survey of that record, drawn with the calm of an architect's plan and finished with meticulous, careful craft.

Space is one flat field seen straight down. There is no perspective and no horizon, only a single slab floating on a quiet ground and lifted by one soft, offset shadow. Inside the slab, geometry is strict: parallel bays at a constant pitch, one perfect circle, one dead-straight lane. Every interval is measured, so the eye trusts the drawing before it reads anything.

Colour is limited and mineral: a deep slate field, a darker slab, bone-white paint. Two signal colours are used sparingly and only where the ground gives an instruction. Hazard yellow marks where not to stop. Cone orange marks the one hidden route. Nothing is a gradient for effect; tone changes only where paint or rubber would really change it.

Rhythm comes from repetition: bay after bay, cone after cone, ring inside ring of faint rubber where tyres circled for hours. These accumulated marks are drawn by hand-tuned rules, with slightly different weight and opacity each time, so the surface reads as used, not printed. Every one of these marks should look placed by an expert with painstaking patience.

Text is a single quiet measurement in a corner, set small, like a scale note on a survey sheet. The composition keeps its lower third calm so that other information can sit over it. The result should feel like the product of deep expertise: a precise, beautiful diagram of an ordinary place.
